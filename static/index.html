How it works

  1. Projection. A gradient-boosted model per stat, trained on every game since 2015-16. It learns from recent and long-run form, minutes, rest, schedule, experience, time with team, the opponent's defense as of that date, and shot-zone and play-type matchups. Until it's trained, a hand-built formula model is used instead.
  2. No peeking. Every training row uses only what was known before tip-off, and the most recent season is held out and scored once.
  3. Distribution. Negative binomial around the projection using the player's own game-to-game spread, widened for new teams and early-season uncertainty.
  4. Calibration. Over/under probabilities are recalibrated per stat on a validation season, so a stated 60% hits about 60% of the time.
  5. Line shopping. Every book's line and price is evaluated; the best one among your books is the recommendation.
  6. EV. Blend model and no-vig market probabilities, compare to the payout, bet only above the EV bar (higher for low-confidence spots).

How it works

  1. Projection. One gradient-boosted model per market, trained on every NFL game since 2013 (nflverse): recent form, role (target, carry, pass and snap shares), efficiency, game script (spread and implied team points), what the opponent allows to the player's position, and teammates out.
  2. No peeking. Every row uses only what was known before kickoff; 2025 is held out and scored once.
  3. Distribution. Counts: negative binomial. Yards: the out-of-sample spread of actual vs projected. Touchdowns: a calibrated probability. P(over) is recalibrated out of sample.
  4. Decision. Same EV maths as NBA, leaning harder on the market (60%) with a 5% minimum EV.
  5. Paper trading. Every hour the cloud scores every open line and logs it at four checkpoints (first seen, 24h, 2h, closing), then settles it against the box score.

Paper trading

Picks logged by the cloud before kickoff, settled at their own line and price. CLV: how far the market's fair (no-vig) probability moved toward the pick by the close, at the same book and line, in percentage points. It's about 0 at the closing checkpoint by design; consistently positive CLV at earlier checkpoints is the earliest sign of a real edge. When the line itself moved, the direction of the move is counted instead.
